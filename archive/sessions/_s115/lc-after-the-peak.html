Long calls: after the option peaked and gave back 20 points

Each panel is one position's option P&L as % of the debit paid. The peak is the highest mark HELM journaled; the event is the first day the option sat 20+ points below it. The solid line is HELM's own marks (midpoint, up to three a day); the dashed continuation is the same contract's daily price from IBKR after HELM stopped watching — so the question "did the option get back to its high" is asked of the option itself, out to expiry or to Friday 09-04.

Reading it. Five of fifteen got back above the peak — every one of them while HELM was still watching, and four of the five did it within a week of the decline (AAPL took five weeks). Four did not and are done or as good as done (GOOG and TSLA expired below; UNH and DAL sit at −59% and −79% with twelve days left). BAC-real touched the peak on an intraday print and never closed above it. Five are still running with 75–131 days to expiry — all currently below the peak, MA by 28 points. Three "recoveries" in an earlier cut of this study were W64's sign-inverted paper rows read upside down (PNC, PFE, BAC-paper peaks that were troughs); this version recomputes every P&L from the option price and the actual entry, which is also what surfaced TSLA and DAL. Both peak and recovery are marked at midpoint, so W166's bias cancels. Small sample, rising market; re-run in a down month. The four August-expiry contracts no longer exist in IBKR's database, so those panels end where HELM's journal ends.
