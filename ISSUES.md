**HELM-129 · `DOCS` · `OPEN` · the model's parameters are conventions, not validated values -- and the document that said otherwise was built on blogs** -- `claude/HELM-model-vs-best-practices.md` (s79) grades HELM's entry parameters '✅ on target' against a Substack post, two app marketing blogs and traderc.com, and has been read as validation ever since. Superseded for anything load-bearing by **`claude/HELM-evidence-standard.md`** (s90), which grades every parameter A (evidence) / B (convention) / C (folklore) with primary sources.
**The finding that matters:** the variance risk premium -- the reason to sell premium at all -- is documented mainly on INDEX options. Bakshi & Kapadia (2003) measure it at roughly **half the size on single names** (delta-hedged loss 0.03% of underlying vs 0.07% for SPX; ~8.65% of call price vs ~16%). And Chicago Fed WP 2025-17 finds it has **converged to zero on the index since about 2012** -- information ratios from -0.6 to +0.09, cumulative traded-put return zero from March 2009 to December 2022 -- though that paper covers index only and cannot be read directly onto single names. **HELM sells single-name premium exclusively.** That reframes the -$39,211: the book may be mis-sized and under-managed on a thin edge rather than mis-calibrated, which is exactly the shape W15's numbers showed.
**Consequences, in priority order:** stop treating entry parameters as validated (label them convention in the register); gate the sell side on IV-vs-realized, not only IV Rank, since that is where the Tier-A literature is and s90 already put the measure on the board; **finish the learning loop (HELM-023) -- if the external edge is half-size and possibly decayed, the book is the only authority left**; fix sizing (W12), because severity dominates when the edge is thin.
**Where HELM is ahead of the standard, and should not be 'corrected':** no mechanical stop on short premium (s90 produced direct book evidence -- a -50% stop replay gave 3 saves and 3 whipsaws), exiting on information rather than price, the ownership scorer, and capturing the counterfactual at all.
